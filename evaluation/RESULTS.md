| Comparison | Measure | n | mean (first group) | mean (second group) | difference | Cohen's d | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| name origin: South Asian vs Western | overall score (0-10) | 20 / 20 | 6.95 | 7 | -0.05 | -0.096 | 1.0 |
| name origin: South Asian vs Western | share of recommendations Hire or Strong Hire | 20 / 20 | 0.95 | 0.85 |  |  | 0.605 |

Smallest p-value across the 6 comparisons: 0.234. With that many comparisons, p below about 0.01 would be needed to suspect a real effect (Bonferroni-style caution). No comparison does, so this experiment found no statistically detectable name or gender effect.

Limitations: one role, one 3-answer transcript of medium quality, one CV, four name/gender combinations and one pronoun pair (he/him, she/her); the recommendation step sees only evaluator text, not the CV, so it can only inherit a difference from the evaluations; repeats are independent model samples (temperature 0.7), not different people. With 20 repeats per group, only fairly large effects (roughly Cohen's d of 0.9 or more, at 80% power) could be detected; a smaller bias would not show up. Absence of a detectable difference here is not proof of fairness for other roles, strengths or demographic signals.
